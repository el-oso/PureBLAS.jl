# The lint fails BOTH ways: a pair not listed here is a new finding, and a line here that no longer occurs
# is stale (a removed caller must not leave a rubber stamp behind). Regenerate the raw list with
#   julia test/workspace_lint.jl --baseline
# and then re-justify every line by READING it. A line whose reason you cannot restate is not a pass.
#
# The lint is name-level and conservative: it treats a claim as live to the end of the enclosing method and
# merges all methods of a name, so sequential re-claims, mutually exclusive if/ternary arms, and
# claim-once-then-thread-it-down all show up here. That is the intended trade — over-approximate, then
# justify in writing, exactly as req8_lint.jl does with its literals.

# (gemm.jl:2844) and its `_gemm_blocked!` call (:2858) are the two arms of one `if _use_unpacked`.

gpackA  _gemm_core! -> _gemm_core!
gpackB  _gemm_core! -> _gemm_blocked!
gpackB  _gemm_core! -> _gemm_core!

# --- Higham-Hager estimator, DECLARED callback edge (_CALLBACK_EDGES). `_lacn2!` claims lacn* once at
# entry and drives `applyop!` in a loop; the closure (gecon!/trcon!/pocon!) runs trsv!/getrs!/potrs!, none
# of which claims a lacn* field. The cycle here is the declared edge closing back on the caller, not a
# second claim. This is the one pair whose safety rests on the closures, so re-read it if a NEW caller of
# _lacn2! appears — that is precisely what the declared edge is for.

# --- potrf pad: `_potrf_gen!`, `_potrf_upper_lever_real!` and `_potrf_upper_lever_cmplx!` are three

# --- Strassen pool: `str` is ONE field holding a POOL, and the re-entrancy is by design —

# (workspace.jl:368-387), not the field split, so a field-keyed lint cannot adjudicate it and must not try.
